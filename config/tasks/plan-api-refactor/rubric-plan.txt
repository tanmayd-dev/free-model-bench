Score each dimension from 1 (unusable) to 10 (exceptional). Be exacting: a
plan that sounds architectural but does not engage with the code actually in
the repository should not score above 5 on any dimension.

DIMENSIONS

codebase_reading
  1-3  Describes a generic layered rewrite. Does not name the files or
       functions that exist, or names ones that do not.
  4-6  Names `api.py`, `db.py` and `auth.py` and gestures at their contents,
       but the mapping to specific functions is missing.
  7-8  Every function that moves is named, and the plan correctly identifies
       what `handle`, `_route`, `db.query`, `auth.SESSIONS` and
       `_list_products` actually do today.
  9-10 All of the above, plus the plan catches details that only reading the
       code reveals -- e.g. that `_split` strips trailing slashes, that the
       `q` parameter is interpolated into SQL while other parameters are
       bound, that `sku` matching is case-normalised in `_create_product`
       but not in `_get_product`, or that `_parse_body` maps malformed JSON
       to 400 via a bare `except (ValueError, ...)`.

migration_soundness
  1-3  Big-bang move, or a sequence where intermediate states do not run.
  4-6  Incremental in outline, but the steps are not independently
       verifiable or the tree is broken between them.
  7-8  Ordered steps that each leave `tests/` green, with the verification
       for each step named and the target layout reached.
  9-10 Steps are small enough to land individually, dependencies between
       them are explicit, the `handle` shim is carried across rather than
       cut over, and the plan says what to do when a step turns out to be
       bigger than expected.

seam_coverage
  1-3  Ignores the obstacles the brief calls out.
  4-6  Mentions connection-per-query and sessions but offers no mechanism
       for unwinding them without changing behaviour.
  7-8  Addresses the connection churn, the process-local `SESSIONS`, the
       order-sensitive route chain and the `handle` entry point, each with a
       concrete technique.
  9-10 Above plus the harder seams: `db.execute` returning
       `last_insert_rowid()` immediately (so it cannot become a transaction
       boundary without changing what callers see), `connect()` re-running
       `executescript` on every call, and `sqlite3.Row` leaking into
       service-layer return types.

risk_and_verification
  1-3  "Run the tests" is the whole verification story.
  4-6  Names the test suite and one or two risks, with no detection method.
  7-8  A concrete behaviour-preservation strategy (characterisation tests,
       response-body snapshots, error-string assertions) plus a risk
       register with blast radius and detection.
  9-10 Above, and the plan explicitly covers the behaviours the visible
       suite does NOT pin down -- the exact JSON key order, the `LIMIT`
       interpolation, 409 vs 400 on duplicate SKUs, and the stock-deduction
       race -- while still refusing to change any of them in this pass.

structure
  1-3  Wall of prose, or missing whole sections the brief demands.
  4-6  Organised loosely; sections present but thin or duplicated.
  7-8  All seven required sections, internally consistent, scannable.
  9-10 Complete and self-contained: an engineer who has never seen the repo
       could execute it, and any one answer can be found in seconds.

LENGTH IS NOT SCORED. A shorter plan that engages with the real code beats a
long one that does not. Do not reward verbosity, formatting flourish, or
hedging. Do not reward a plan for proposing behaviour changes -- those are
explicitly out of scope and must be flagged, not smuggled in.
